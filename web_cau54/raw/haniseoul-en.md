## How to Write a 700-Character TOPIK II Question 54 Essay: Structuring Your Introduction, Body, and Conclusion
A comprehensive guide to writing the TOPIK II Question 54 long-form essay, which carries the highest weight (50 points). This guide covers drafting opening sentences, solving the three required prompts, developing body paragraphs, using formal register, and meeting the 600–700 character count.

The 'Question 54 long-form essay (50 points)' is the final and most difficult part of the TOPIK II Writing section. It is notorious for leaving students blank-faced or unable to finish on time. You must answer three specific prompts regarding a complex modern issue while producing a logical, well-structured essay of 600–700 characters. Here, we outline a four-paragraph framework and formatting rules to help you achieve a high score of 40 or above.

## Question 54 Grading Criteria and the Three Pillars of a High Score

According to the National Institute for International Education's grading guidelines, Question 54 is evaluated on three core pillars: 'Content and Task Completion (thoroughly answering the three prompts),' 'Essay Structure and Logical Flow (paragraph division and connective endings),' and 'Vocabulary and Grammar Variety (using advanced vocabulary and complex sentence structures).'

The key to a passing grade is providing clear, balanced answers for each of the three prompts (approx. 150–180 characters each). Neglecting one prompt or providing only a one-line answer will result in a significant deduction for failing to complete the task.

Controlling your word count is absolutely critical to success on Question 54. Writing fewer than 600 characters triggers an automatic deduction of over 30% of your score, and anything written outside the manuscript grid is not graded. You must practice until you can reliably end your essay within the 650-character range (33–35 lines of the manuscript).

## 10-Minute Brainstorming: Strategy for Building Your Framework

If you start writing immediately upon receiving the test, your logic will likely tangle around the 300-character mark, causing you to waste time erasing and rewriting. You must spend 5–8 minutes jotting down an 'outline' in the margins of your paper.

Topic Analysis: Define the keyword and its importance in modern society.
Note two core arguments and supporting keywords for Question 1.
Note two causes and side effects for Question 2.
Note two practical individual or societal solutions for Question 3.
Once this framework is in place, 20–25 minutes is enough to fill the actual manuscript.

Looking at the time management habits of high-scoring candidates, they spend a full 8–10 minutes of their 35-minute allowance for Question 54 purely on outlining. By noting down 'one core argument + one specific piece of evidence + one realistic example' as keywords for each of the three prompts, you can ensure logical consistency and keep your pen moving without interruption while writing your essay.

## Standard 4-Part Structure Template: Introduction-Body 1-Body 2-Conclusion

A perfect 600–700-character essay should be divided into exactly 3–4 paragraphs.

Paragraph 1 (Introduction, ~100 characters): Define the topic generally and raise the issue of why this discussion is important in modern society. "In modern society, [Topic] is emerging as a critical factor that determines the quality of life."
Paragraph 2 (Body 1, ~200 characters): Answer the first question on the prompt by describing the positive aspects or the necessity of the phenomenon.
Paragraph 3 (Body 2, ~250 characters): Provide an in-depth analysis of the second question, such as its problems, negative impacts, or causes.
Paragraph 4 (Conclusion, ~150 characters): Address the third question by suggesting concrete solutions and summarizing the future direction society should take.

When writing the conclusion, it is exemplary to offer a 'two-way solution' that synthesizes the problems mentioned earlier, suggesting that individual attitude improvements must work in tandem with national or societal policy support. Complete your essay with a mature sentence such as: "True coexistence can only be achieved when individual voluntary efforts are accompanied by effective legal regulations and infrastructure from the government."

## Advanced Grammar and Connector Strategy for Essays

To elevate the quality of your writing, you should actively use academic complex sentence endings instead of simple enumerative sentences (e.g., "~and, ~so").

Causes/Evidence: "Judging from ~," "It can be attributed to ~"

Concession/Contrast: "Despite ~," "On the other hand, ~"

Condition/Assumption: "Cannot rule out the case that ~," "It is natural that ~"

Conclusion/Recommendation: “~must,” “~should devote every effort to” Intentionally incorporating one or two of these advanced grammatical devices in each paragraph can leave the grader with a strong impression of your academic Korean proficiency.

Furthermore, you should train yourself to naturally incorporate advanced socio-humanistic vocabulary such as 'desirable,' 'must be avoided,' 'cause side effects,' and 'establish institutional mechanisms.' The grader evaluates not only whether your sentences are grammatically correct but also your overall ability to view sharp contemporary debates from an intellectual and balanced perspective in Korean.

## Taboo Practices That Lead to Deductions and Manuscript Formatting Rules

Prohibit first-person subjective expressions: Instead of subjective terms like "I think" or "In my view," use objective descriptors such as "Generally" or "According to social convention."

Prohibit colloquialisms: Do not end sentences with '-oyo'; consistently finish them with '-da/-neunda'.

Beware of insufficient length: Falling short of 600 characters results in a severe point deduction, and sentences exceeding 700 characters that overflow the manuscript paper will be excluded from grading.
